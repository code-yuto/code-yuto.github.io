Yuto Watanabe

Information and Communications Engineering, College of Engineering, Shibaura Institute of Technology (expected graduation: March 2028)

At university I have built things in teams across very different settings: gPBL projects with overseas universities, ShibaLab, a digital art club that creates interactive art and other works, and a long-term internship. Each team had different members, languages and goals.

When I first started team development, I did not know how to divide the work or move a project forward. Curious about how software is actually developed in industry, I passed the Fundamental Information Technology Engineer Examination during the spring break of my first year. I am now working on the Applied Information Technology Engineer Examination (passed Part A; Part B upcoming).

Things did not go well from the start. Systems failed on the day, and ideas did not get across. I reviewed each failure and changed how I worked so that I would not repeat it. That steady improvement led to winning the top award as team leader at gPBL in summer 2026.

What excites me is making things that keep running in the real world without stopping. That is why I want to work on the infrastructure and networks underneath them.

gPBL (Global Project Based Learning)

Best Award

Drought Monitoring System

gPBL Program on IoT in the Era of LLMs 2026 / Aug–Sep 2026 / Hanoi, Vietnam

An IoT system that measures soil moisture in the field and shows drought risk with a four-color LED and a dashboard. Green means normal, yellow medium risk, red high risk, and purple a sensor fault.

Building on my hardware and IoT system design work in the previous gPBL, I led the team this time. I divided the roles, kept the project on track, summarized our results in English and presented them. We won the top award.

TRACE

Shibaura Festival 2026

An interactive exhibit that recreates the Toyosu Campus buildings in 3D and sends a beam of light through them. At the end, each visitor sees “your trace” on their own smartphone.

I built the exhibit screen and the 3D models of the campus. Using location data collected on the front end, I applied graph theory and shortest-path search to draw routes on the map.

Gensō Shinkidō

ARD (Hosei University) × ShibaLab / 2026

An immersive exhibit created together with ARD at Hosei University. In a dark room, visitors point a flashlight at the wall, and images appear only where the light falls, revealing puzzle clues and scenery.

I designed the system. I built the mechanism that shows images only in the direction the flashlight points, and projected onto two walls instead of one to create a sense of depth. I put particular effort into the transitions between scenes.
